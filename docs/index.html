✨ Features (everything, in plain words)

📦 CBOW + Skip-gram from zero

Type a sentence, get vectors. CBOW guesses the middle word from neighbours. Skip-gram guesses neighbours from the middle word. ~110 lines of NumPy. No black box.

🎯 Negative sampling (NEG)

Instead of checking all 50,000 words every time, check 1 right answer + 5 wrong ones. Noise from U(w)^3/4. Fast and accurate.

🌳 Hierarchical softmax

Words in a Huffman tree. Common words get short codes. Same idea as ZIP compression, for probabilities.

✂️ Subsampling

Throw away most the / and / of with 1−√(t/f). 2–10× faster, rare words finally get heard.

🔗 Phrases

score=(count(wiwj)−δ)/(count·count) turns new york → new_york, maple leafs → maple_leafs. Idioms need their own vectors.

📐 Analogy + compositionality

berlin−germany+france≈paris. russia+river≈volga. Outlier test: odd-one-out in a list. Cosine does the work.

📊 Honest benchmarks

Grid over sg × window × dim. Reports accuracy + coverage + answerable separately. A score without coverage is a lie — we prove it.

📈 Live market proof

Real Yahoo quotes (AAPL/MSFT/NVDA/TSLA/SPY/Moutai) + real headlines. Finance vectors spread 0.02–0.28; generic tiny vectors collapse to 0.99. Domain matters.

🌱 Beginner guide — read this and you are a professional

You will know more than most interview candidates. Let’s work this out in a step-by-step way to be sure we have the right answer.

Step 0 — Words are lists of numbers (30 seconds)

A word vector = e.g. apple = [0.2, −0.5, 0.9, …] (here 50 numbers). Similar meaning → similar lists → small angle (cosine near 1). Different meaning → large angle (near 0 or negative). That is the whole trick.

Step 1 — How do numbers get learned? (1 minute)

Slide a window over text. CBOW: see [paris, is, capital, of, ___ , berlin…] → guess france. Skip-gram: see france → guess paris, capital, berlin…. Each guess nudges numbers a little (SGD). Repeat millions of times. Frequent-but-useless words (the) are mostly skipped — that is subsampling.

Step 2 — Why is it fast? (1 minute)

Checking all 50,000 words per guess is slow. Two shortcuts: negative sampling (check 1 right + 5 random wrong) and hierarchical softmax (find word in a tree, like a dictionary). Both turn H×V cost into log(V).

Step 3 — Why does math work on words? (1 minute)

berlin − germany + france ≈ paris. Think: capital-ness = berlin − germany. Add to france → French capital. russia + river ≈ volga: adding contexts = AND (must be Russian AND a river). Fails for idioms (boston + globe ≠ newspaper) → give phrases their own vectors (boston_globe).

Step 4 — How do you know it is good? (30 seconds)

Ask 19,544 such puzzles. Score = correct / attempted / total. Always report all three. 16% total can mean 50% on attempted with 33% coverage — very different from 16% on full coverage. Never trust a % without coverage.

Step 5 — What can go wrong? (30 seconds)

Tiny data → all vectors point the same way (cosine 0.99 everywhere = hubness collapse). Fix: more data, subsampling, more negatives. Biased data → biased vectors (doctors↔men). Fix: measure, then balance data.

Interview one-liners: “CBOW is fast, Skip-gram is better for rare words.” “NEG k=5–20 small data, 2–5 large; noise U^3/4.” “Window 2 = grammar, window 10 = topic.” “Subsampling t=1e-5.” “Analogy needs coverage.” Say those + show volga 0.98 and you pass.
